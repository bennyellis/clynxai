  matcher made 47 false matches among the 75 pairs of products it called "the same" (63% wrong), and 18 wrong picks among the 230 listings. It does this by being

  apply all 39 data changes, undo all, and the database is byte-identical; undo in two halves; a row you edited by hand after an apply is never overwritten by an undo;

Undo mechanism (audit/phase4_shadow/correction_journal.ts, NOT wired into the app):
  - only one-row updates of named columns (listing / photo product, a valuation, a candidate's status); it never inserts into data tables or deletes anything;
  - each change and its journal line (full prior value) are written in ONE transaction;
  - an item whose row no longer holds what the plan expected is skipped, not forced; an undo whose row was edited since is refused, not forced;
  - undo works for a whole batch, for any subset, and newest-first.
Round trip on throwaway copies (stage6a_roundtrip.ts):
  apply 39 changes -> database changed -> undo -> byte-identical in every affected table (products, listings, photos incl. image bytes, relations, aliases): PASS
  undo in two halves -> intermediate state differs, final state identical: PASS
  applying the same plan twice: second time applies nothing: PASS
  a row edited by hand after the apply: undo refuses that row, keeps your edit, undoes the other 38: PASS
  stale plan (prior values wrong): applies nothing: PASS
  a change to a table or column that is not allowed: refused: PASS

7. REMAINING LIMITATIONS OF UNIVERSAL PRODUCT IDENTIFICATION
======================================================================
 1. The ground truth is mine: one reviewer (the assistant), 14 truly-same product pairs, 156+74 listings, mostly video games and phones. 398 of the 551 reviewed pairs were
    labelled "different" by rule (the foundation called them related and neither older matcher called them the same) and checked by a random sample of 40 (39 clearly
    right, 1 ambiguous). Please treat every number as an estimate; a person should look at the 14 true pairs and the difficult examples in section 4.
 2. I tuned the resolver while reading this copy's results. "0 false matches" is therefore measured on data it was fitted to. The held-out tests were written before
    running, but they are 21 checks, not a study.
 3. Recall on duplicates is low by design (2 of 14 confirmed). A leading word (maker, "new", "handmade") is ambiguous: "Apple iPhone 11" and "New 3DS XL" look alike
    to a program and are opposite cases. It leans on 3 pairs and is wrong on 1.
 4. It depends on the Database's own names being decent. Where the names are contaminated (a placeholder "iPhone (standard model smartphone)", an entry named "xr",
    an entry named "Gaming PC -") the answers are only as good as the entry.
 5. Evidence for "this word separates products" comes from the Database itself (entries kept in pairs: X and X+word, at least 4 places = firm, 2-3 = a lean). In a
    Database with few such pairs it says "uncertain". It uses word position (after the shared name counts, in front does not) to avoid treating makers as variants.
 6. A title that omits a widely shared word is accepted only when exactly one plain word is missing and the model markers are present; titles that omit more, typos
    ("Swich"), plurals ("dresses"), other languages ("Vendo. Xbox serie nuevo") and abbreviations nobody else uses resolve to "uncertain".
 7. Several products in one title, bundles and lots: it names the main item and lists the others, or says uncertain; it does not price a lot.
 8. The relation label is sometimes imprecise (a sequel typed "variant"; "Dying Light 2" against "Dying Light") although the verdict "not the same" is right.
 9. The audit cannot see individual sold comparables, only the stored average and size; and it cannot audit the 400 entries with no filed material.
10. Condition, authenticity, region and seller trust are outside identification and untouched.

10. RECOMMENDATION: IS ANY LIVE MATCHING BEHAVIOUR READY?
======================================================================
NOT READY (needs your explicit authorisation and more evidence first):
  - using the resolver to confirm that two products are the same (alias promotion, merging, deduplication): it confirms too few to be useful and its "lean" is wrong 1 time in 3;
  - creating or renaming products from its output; letting it decide a listing's product for valuation or alerts as the only authority;
  - any automatic correction of the Database (the plan and journal are tested, but only on copies).
CLOSEST TO READY - a "veto only" use, if you choose to authorise it as the next stage:
  Keep today's matcher as it is, but never let its "same product" answer stand when the resolver says DIFFERENT, RELATED (goes-with / variant / bundle / family) or
  UNCERTAIN-leaning-different. Measured on your copy, of the old matcher's 75 "same" calls: that veto would have stopped 41 of the 47 false product merges, would
  also have stopped 16 of the 19 ambiguous ones and 2 of the 9 right ones (those stay as two entries - the safe error, nothing is lost). The 6 false merges it would
  NOT stop are all "uncertain" cases (for example "Nintendo 3DS LL" = "Nintendo 3DS"); letting a call stand only when the resolver confirms it or leans "same" would
  stop 46 of 47 but also 7 of the 9 right ones. For listings the better use is the resolver's own pick: of the old matcher's 18 wrong filings it picks the right entry for 9 and abstains on
  the other 9 (none still wrong), and of the 127 right ones it keeps 119 and leaves 8 unconfirmed. I would roll any of this out in shadow-to-live steps: log the
  decisions for one scan cycle, you review them, then switch it on behind a flag, for comparable-sale filtering and listing filing only, with the Phase 4 stage 1 rule
  that an unverified identifier never starts research or suppresses a deal unchanged.
Before that, I recommend: (1) you look at the section 4 examples and the 14 true pairs and tell me where my labels are wrong; (2) the 10 different-product findings are
reviewed by you in the private findings file - they are real filing errors today and the plan's 17 photo moves and 2 listing moves are the lowest-risk fixes (still
only on your approval); (3) the Database is fed more non-game, non-phone products before the resolver's category-independence is claimed from data rather than tests.
