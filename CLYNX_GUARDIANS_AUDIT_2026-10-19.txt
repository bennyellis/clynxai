CLYNX AUDIT - how "xbox series x galaxy edition" pulls in "Guardians of the Galaxy"
2026-10-19. Auditor session. Read-only: no repo or user data changed. I ran the app's own matching code (bundled into the scratchpad,
not the repo) on real titles, queried the pre-reset database read-only, and loaded one WatchCount page in the built-in browser.
VERIFIED = I ran it or saw it. INFERRED = follows from it.

SHORT ANSWER
The game shares three words with the console name ("Galaxy", "Edition", "Xbox Series X"). Clynx tries to tell a game from a console
by reading the TITLE FORMAT with pattern rules. That was patched in Beta 12 for one exact title shape, and it breaks on the next one.
For the name you typed, "Xbox Series X Galaxy Edition", a Guardians game title is scored an EXACT match, as a photo and as a sold price.


1. THE FIVE WAYS IT GETS IN

W1  The title test only recognises a few title shapes                                                  VERIFIED (ran the real code)
    Product "Xbox Series X Galaxy Edition" (what you typed):
      "Guardians of the Galaxy (Xbox Series X) ..."  platform in brackets            -> rejected   (the Beta 12 fix)
      "Marvel's Guardians ... Cosmic Deluxe Edition Xbox Series X"                    -> rejected
      "Guardians of the Galaxy Xbox Series X Brand New Factory Sealed"                -> photo MATCH, sold-price EXACT
      "Guardians of the Galaxy - Xbox Series X - Brand New"                           -> photo MATCH, sold-price EXACT
      "Marvel Guardians ... Cosmic Deluxe Edition Xbox Series X Complete in Box"      -> photo MATCH, sold-price EXACT
    Product "Xbox Series X Galaxy Black Edition": the same three titles come out "insufficient" (photo) and "compatible" (price).
    "Compatible" means nothing contradicts it; those prices can be counted. The old word-count test also says yes to all of them.
    Why: mediaSignal() (productKind.ts) spots a game only when the platform is in brackets, or at the very end followed by a short list
    of words (new, sealed, used...). A dash before "Brand New", or "Factory Sealed", or "Complete in Box" slips past it.

W2  Other game titles pass the same way (this is TESTER's wrong-photo finding)                         VERIFIED
    Product "Xbox Series X Starfield":  "Starfield - Xbox Series X - Brand New" -> MATCH; "Starfield Xbox Series X Brand New Factory
    Sealed" -> MATCH. Product "Xbox Series X Halo Infinite": "Halo Infinite Xbox Series X Xbox One Brand New Factory Sealed" -> MATCH.
    Those are the exact eBay titles saved on the Starfield and Halo Infinite entries, which is why their photos are game box art.
    A product NAME that contains the game word makes every game word in the title agree, so the title looks like an exact match.

W3  Save-credits mode lets the "compatible" ones file without a picture check                          VERIFIED (code, photoIdentity.ts)
    fileableWithoutPicture() accepts "match" or "insufficient + compatible". Your lowAiMode is off now, so a picture check should run,
    but anyone with Save-credits on (the default) gets game photos filed under console entries.

W4  The product record itself gets created for the game                                                VERIFIED (data) / INFERRED (cause)

    "Xbox Series X Galaxy Edition" (00:50:30), filed under Xbox > Games > Xbox Series X. No listing is linked to it. Its sold average

2. WHAT THIS TELLS US
- The sold search is not the main polluter for this phrase: WatchCount's own ranking returns the right consoles. The pollution comes
  from what Clynx does around it: name matching, title format guessing, the research that follows, and product creation.
- "Is this a game, a console or an accessory?" is decided by title wording. Titles are written by hundreds of different sellers, so
  any pattern list will keep missing shapes. This is the same weakness behind the console accessory filter (hdmi / controller
  rejecting real consoles, database audit F2): one rule set trying to guess kind from words.
- The game and console problems are two ends of one issue: Clynx has no stored fact for "what kind of thing is this entry, and what
  edition is it". The identity card in CLYNX_PRODUCT_IDENTITY_AUDIT (D3) is the missing piece.


3. RECOMMENDED FIXES (design only, for DEVELOPER; nothing done)
G1  Decide kind from structure, not wording. Use the eBay category the listing already carries (Video Game Consoles vs Video Games vs
    Accessories) for comps and photos, and the Facebook category/AI kind for listings. Only fall back to title patterns when there is no
    category. WatchCount's search URL has an empty category slot ("/sold/<words>/-/fixedprice"); fill it for consoles.
G2  Store the kind on the product (console / game / accessory) in the identity card; then a game can never be "exact" for a console entry,
    whatever its title looks like, and a game entry can never take console prices.
G3  Make "compatible" not enough to file a photo or count a price when the name carries an edition word or the title names a different
    franchise. At minimum: a title with extra distinctive words not in the product name (Guardians, Marvel, Cosmic Deluxe, Starfield) is
    "uncertain", not exact, when the product name is a console.
G4  Extend mediaSignal: platform name preceded by a dash or followed by any condition words (Factory Sealed, Complete in Box, CIB, Brand New);
    add the tests above as regression cases (they currently pass the wrong way).
G5  Stop product creation from names that look like games when the trigger was a console search. Log WHY each product was created
    (source and triggering text) so this can be traced next time.
G6  Match listings to the most specific product (Galaxy Black) and, when only a generic parent has a value, flag the listing as
    "edition unpriced" instead of rejecting it as a loss. Never apply a plain-console value to a special edition.
G7  Regression suite: the real titles from this audit (7 console titles, 5 Guardians variants, Starfield, Halo) against the product names
    "Xbox Series X Galaxy Edition", "...Galaxy Black Edition", "...Starfield", "...Halo Infinite".
