FEATURE SPEC - "ADD-ONS" under Variants in the Library (owner request, 2026-10-19)
AUDITOR draft for DEVELOPER. DESIGN ONLY: the owner has NOT said go on any code. Sized as a LARGE item: it depends on the identity card (D3) and the comps fixes (D4) and should be built after them.

=====================================================================
0. THE HUMAN THOUGHT, CORRECTED
=====================================================================
The reseller's question is not "is everything included?" but "WHAT ALL is included?" It works in two directions:
  (a) Missing expected parts LOWER the value: a Galaxy console with no controller, no cables or no box sells for less than a complete one.
  (b) Extra parts can be hidden gems: a 1TB expansion card, a second controller, a game bundle, a charging dock. A $700 listing that holds a $1,100 console plus a $150 card is an "insane deal".
Clynx already looks for (b) (hidden gems), but with fixed guesses. It has nothing for (a). This feature teaches Clynx both from real sales.

=====================================================================
1. WHAT THE OWNER ASKED FOR (expanded)
=====================================================================
1.1 A new section called "Add-ons" under Variants on a Library product page (for example the Galaxy edition Xbox). An add-on is a thing that can come WITH the base product: controller, original box, expansion card, dock, cables, a game.
1.2 Each add-on is its own Database entry (researched, with its own identity card, photos, comps), linked to the base product.
1.3 When Clynx meets a familiar base product in a listing and sees an add-on (familiar or new), it automatically creates an Add-on option on that base product, which the user can toggle on and off.
1.4 Toggling an add-on ON raises the estimated profit the user sees.
1.5 The raise is NOT linear. Clynx looks at eBay sold listings and compares selling the add-on alone, selling the base alone, and selling them together, then uses what the data shows. Example from the owner: base $400, the add-on sells for $100 alone but bundled listings average $450, so the bundled uplift is about $50, not $100.
1.6 Be smart, not overpromising: show how sure Clynx is, and never present a guess as a fact.
1.7 Clynx researches each new add-on and finds related add-ons (things that fit the same base).

=====================================================================
2. WHAT EXISTS TODAY (so this builds on it, not beside it)
=====================================================================
- bundleBreakdown.ts: looksLikeBundle(), breakDownListing() (one AI call splits a listing into items), valueBundle(). Its numbers are FIXED GUESSES: accessory weight 0.55, extra weight 0.45, extra units x0.5, total uplift soft-capped at 30% of the main item (or 50% of the raw sum). Nothing is learned from sales.
- profitGate.ts: isHiddenGemCandidate, repriceWithExtras (fee-aware), extrasPricedSeparately ("sold separately", "$15 each" means the extras are NOT in the price: no hidden-gem value). Keep that rule.
- scrapeEngine.ts: bundle valuation only for deals / hidden-gem near-misses / Watch candidates; index.ts valueListingBundle prices each extra via checkMarketBeforeAlert.
- Library already has "Specific models" (children, parent_product_id) and "Variants" siblings. There is NO add-on concept and NO table for it.
- No learning of what is "frequently a good add-on".

=====================================================================
3. DATA MODEL (new, additive)
=====================================================================
product_addons
  id, base_product_id, addon_product_id (its own products row, kind = accessory/addon),
  role: expected | bonus   (expected = normally included, missing lowers value; bonus = extra, present raises value)
  status: suggested | confirmed | dismissed   (created automatically = suggested; the user can confirm or dismiss)
  source: seen_in_listing | researched_related | user_added
  times_seen, first_seen_at, last_seen_at, example_listing_ids
  compatibility: verified | plausible | unknown   (a 1TB expansion card fits Series X|S, not a PS5)
  stats_json (see 4): standalone_median, standalone_n, bundled_median, base_only_median, premium_usd, premium_n, premium_low/high, confidence, checked_at
listing_addons (per scanned listing): listing_id, base_product_id, addon_id, evidence (title / description / photo), confidence
The add-on's own products row follows the identity-card rules (official name, kind, SKU, sources). A listing that is only an add-on (just the card) is an add-on product listing, not a base-product listing (kind gate from D3).

=====================================================================
4. HOW CLYNX LEARNS WHAT AN ADD-ON IS REALLY WORTH (the core)
=====================================================================
Three numbers per add-on, each with its sample size:
  A  standalone sold price: what the add-on sells for alone (sold comps for its own entry).
  B  bundled premium: median sold price of listings of the base WITH the add-on, minus median sold price of the base ALONE, matched on edition, condition and recent date window.
  C  parting-out path: A minus the extra cost of a second sale (second eBay fee at your 84% keep, extra shipping, time to sell).
Method
  1. Pull sold listings for the base product (comps pipeline, identity-gated: Galaxy only, no other generations, no for-parts) and split them by what the TITLE says is included: base only / base + this add-on / base + other things.
  2. Compare medians, not averages, and drop outliers. Require a minimum sample (suggest 5 per group) before B is shown as learned; with fewer, B is "not enough data" and the estimate falls back to the old fixed-weight guess labelled ROUGH.
  3. Never present an uplift larger than the learned premium B when B has enough data. When A is larger than B, show both and say so ("sells alone for about $100, but bundles add about $50"): it is the user's choice whether to part it out.
  4. Several add-ons: do not add them up. Diminishing returns (reuse valueBundle's idea of a cap, but from data); cap the total uplift at what bundled sales show for the full set, and show a range.
  5. Expected parts (role = expected): learn the deduction the same way: base alone vs complete.
  6. Everything stored with sample size, date window and confidence (high >= 10 matched sales, medium 5-9, low < 5 or fallback).
  7. Honest display: ranges, "based on N sold listings", "rough" when it is not from sales, never "profitable now" from an add-on alone (same weak-evidence rule as the owner's Q1 reference basis).
Worked example (owner's numbers): base $400, add-on alone $100. Bundled sales of base+add-on average $450, base alone $400: premium B = $50. Net of the 84% keep: bundled uplift about +$42; parting out about $84 minus a second shipment, minus time. Default estimate shown = the conservative bundled +$42, with the parting-out figure shown next to it.
Real-world check from AUDITOR (WatchCount, first page, "xbox series x galaxy edition"): 20 sold Galaxy Black consoles from $836 to $1,549, middle about $1,100-$1,150. Some titles say "w/ Controller", "Box" or "Complete Box": that is exactly the data to split by inclusions. Clynx cannot do it today because those titles are rejected as accessories (backlog B2) and edition wording splits them (B1).

=====================================================================
5. AUTOMATIC DISCOVERY AND RELATED ADD-ONS
=====================================================================
- On each scanned listing of a KNOWN base product: run the existing breakdown (title, description, photos) and compare the items with that base product's add-ons. Familiar add-on: increment times_seen. Unfamiliar one: create a suggested add-on and queue its research (own entry, card, comps), under the D3 budget and burst rules.
- Noise filter: ignore generic filler ("cables", "manual") until seen in several listings; ignore anything priced separately ("sold separately", "$15 each").
- Related add-ons: ask the identity-card research "what accessories and add-ons fit this exact product", keep only the ones with a source, mark compatibility, create them as suggested (never auto-confirmed).
- Compatibility gate: an add-on only attaches to bases it fits (kind and compatibility from the cards).
- A listing that already includes the add-on gets the uplift applied by the scanner automatically only when the add-on is DETECTED in the listing (title/description/photo) and included in the price.

=====================================================================
6. UI (Library product page)
=====================================================================
- Under Variants: "Add-ons" list. Each row: thumbnail, name, role badge (Expected / Bonus), "alone ~$X (N sold)", "adds ~$Y when bundled (N sold, medium confidence)" or "rough estimate", a toggle, and a source note ("seen in 4 listings", "researched").
- The profit area on the product page shows: base estimate, then "with selected add-ons" as a RANGE, with a one-line why. Toggle off = back to the base number.
- Suggested add-ons are visibly marked "suggested"; the user can confirm, dismiss, or rename. Dismissed ones never come back.
- Alerts (ntfy) name the add-ons found in the listing and the uplift as "+ about $Y (rough)" when applicable.
- Keep it simple: no more than one new section; plain words; follow the owner's UX complaints (fewer buttons, no jargon).

=====================================================================
7. GUARDRAILS (do not overpromise)
=====================================================================
- Never apply an add-on that is not in the listing.
- Never sum add-ons linearly; always show sample size and confidence; label fallback numbers ROUGH.
- Uplift is limited by what bundled sales show; if there is none, use a conservative cap and say so.
- A thin sample (< 5) cannot raise an alert's stars.
- The add-on's own comps are identity-gated like any product (no game boxes standing in for a controller, no other console's accessory).
- Extra lookups are expensive (WatchCount anonymous cap, OpenAI budget): learn add-ons lazily, only those seen several times or worth more than a threshold, cache for weeks.

=====================================================================
8. DEPENDENCIES AND ORDER
=====================================================================
Needs first: D3 identity card (kind, compatibility, official names), D4 comps fixes (edition aliases B1, "w/ controller" as an included extra B2, not-working guard), reliable WatchCount lookups with logging (B3), and the "why was this product created" log (B5). Without those the sold data this feature learns from is mostly rejected.
Phases
  P1  Data model + manual add-ons + UI toggles, numbers labelled ROUGH from the existing fixed weights.
  P2  Detection in listings: auto-create suggested add-ons, familiar/unfamiliar handling, noise filter.
  P3  Learning from sold data: A, B, C and the expected-part deductions with confidence.
  P4  Related add-on research (compatibility-gated).
  P5  Scanner and alert integration, plus a small backtest on past listings to check estimates against real sold prices.

=====================================================================
9. ACCEPTANCE TESTS
=====================================================================
T1 base $400, add-on alone $100, bundled average $450 -> uplift shown about $50 (net about $42), parting-out shown separately, not $100 added.
T2 fewer than 5 bundled sales -> "not enough data", ROUGH label, conservative cap.
T3 two add-ons: the total is below the sum and capped by bundled data.
T4 add-on priced separately in the listing ("card sold separately for $120") -> no uplift.
T5 unfamiliar add-on on a known base -> a suggested add-on is created, research queued within budget, toggle starts OFF.
T6 dismissed add-on never returns.
T7 add-on incompatible with the base (PS5 card on an Xbox) -> never attached.
T8 expected part missing (Galaxy console with no controller) -> deduction from learned data, labelled with N.
T9 add-on listing alone (just the card) is NOT treated as the base product.
T10 toggle on/off updates the profit display only, never the stored base value.
