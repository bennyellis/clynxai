What I did in one paragraph: when the existing matcher picks a Database entry for a listing, an optional safeguard now asks the foundation resolver whether the listing and
that entry are materially different products. If it has reliable evidence that they are, the match is "vetoed": the listing is not filed under that entry, the entry's
price is not used for it, nothing is added to that entry, and nothing else is chosen in its place. If it only has doubt, the match stands, exactly as today. It is switched
by one line in config.json (off / shadow / enforce) and is off. Separately, Database > Review is a new screen that lists suspected problems with the evidence, side-by-side
existing/suggested, the exact data that would change, and an audit history, with Approve/Reject present but switched off.

2. HOW THE VETO INTEGRATES WITH MATCHING, VALUATION AND FILING
======================================================================
Matching: unchanged. The existing matcher still picks an entry from the name the AI extracted. The safeguard looks at that pick afterwards. It never picks anything.

The decision (identity/safeguard.ts), made category-agnostically from the listing title (and the extracted name, which can only add a veto and never confirm, because it is
the very string that picked the entry):
  allow    the title states what the entry states, or the resolver says it is the same product under another entry.
  unsure   everything else that is not provable: a more specific variant of the same family, a bundle, an edition, rare or unfamiliar words, a mangled title, a capacity,
           a model number, a code nobody else uses, two products named together. Treated EXACTLY like allow by every caller. Uncertainty is never proof of a difference.
  veto     only when there is reliable evidence of a materially different product:
             (1) the title is confidently ANOTHER entry and the resolver says the two are different products (a code, number, size or each-side extra differs);
             (2) the title is something that goes WITH the entry (an accessory or a game for it) or the entry is only named as what the item is for;
             (3) the title is another, more specific entry that differs by a single-letter model marker or a word the data itself uses to tell entries apart in several
                 places ("3S" vs "3", "Plus" vs the plain model), never by a capacity;
             (4) one entry clearly leads the title and the matched entry is plainly a different product, or the matched entry is what the leading item goes with.
  Not judged: an entry that stands for a condition (the "(For Parts / Broken)" variants). The reference-photo check never second-guesses a product the user explicitly linked.
  I tried and REMOVED two more rules because they were wrong too often when measured: calling the title a "variant" of its own entry because of a code or word seen in
  other titles (that rule produced 68 of the first 85 photo-title vetoes, and in the 30 I read by hand most were wrong: specs, colours, regions, a model number of the same product), and calling a trailing extra word ("Mark II") a variant (3 false vetoes on
  true duplicates). The limit this leaves is in section 7.

Filing (store.saveScored): a vetoed match is filed under no entry (product_id stays empty). The listing is still saved, still in the Feed, still goes through the Gate.
Valuation (store.getEbayValueForProductName / getEbayValueForTitle, used by the scorer): a vetoed match gives no value (null), exactly what an unknown product gives.
  The Gate then treats the listing like any product the Database has not seen: Worth a look at worst; a deal when the market check prices what it really is (tested).
Evidence for the Gate (store.getProductSignals): a vetoed match offers no signals, so none of that entry's value, sample, age or codes reach the verdict.
Feed display (the "matched product price" on a card): withdrawn for a vetoed match.
Adding to an entry (scrapeEngine): nothing (no note, no price, no photo) is added to the vetoed entry, and NO other entry is created or chosen in its place.
Hard price cap on the entry: a vetoed match cannot trigger it (it can only remove a reason to skip a listing).
Reference photos for the photo check: a vetoed entry's photos are not used for that listing.
Stage 1.1 rule: untouched. A seller's code that nobody else uses is still note-only; it never causes research, never suppresses a deal, and is never a reason to veto
(tested). Gate thresholds: untouched (the real Gate is used in the discovery tests).
Fails open: any error, an entry the resolver does not know yet, an empty title - the match stands. Off means nothing is built, computed or logged (tested: zero cost).
Not covered (stated plainly): the valuation of the "(For Parts / Broken)" variant looks the entry up by name only (no title available there); a watch-for note on an entry is
still passed to the photo check as a hint; the user-initiated product flows in index.ts (adding, merging, chat) do not use listing titles and are untouched.

7. REMAINING LIMITATIONS
======================================================================
 1. Small samples, one reviewer. 6 wrong listing matches, 94 wrong pair directions, 507 photo titles; labels are mine. The false-veto count (0 of 123 labelled cases, 1 of
    507 photos) is an estimate, not a guarantee; a tool-built set mostly from video games and phones.
 2. Recall is modest by design. It blocks about 81% of the wrong matches the existing matcher would make on this data and 3 of 6 on the live-chain listings; the rest are
    left unsure and stand. In a Database with few entries kept in pairs, words like "Plus" or "S" carry no evidence, so more stays unsure.
 3. A "Mark II", an "XL", a dock, a charger: a trailing word alone is not enough, so a console matched to its dock stays unsure.
 4. The 3 of 6 missed listings include mangled titles and a capacity; none is misfiled worse than today.
 5. Leading words (maker, "New", "Handmade") are ambiguous; "New Nintendo 3DS XL" against "Nintendo 3DS XL" is left unsure.
 6. Titles that name several products are sometimes vetoed against the product that is NOT leading; that is intended (a lot is not a single product) but it removes that
    entry's value for the lot.
 7. One photo-title veto in 507 was wrong (a power cord against a power-cable entry). The photo population has no listing-style hand labels beyond my reading.
 8. The review's confirmed/heuristic labels are as good as the resolver; valuation findings can only see the stored average and sample size, not the individual sold comparables;
    400 of 579 entries have nothing filed under them and cannot be audited.
 9. The mode is read at startup (restart after editing config.json); the safeguard never re-files, never creates, never merges; "unsure" listings are valued exactly as before.
10. Not covered: see the end of section 2.

8. RECOMMENDATION: IS A LIMITED LIVE TRIAL SAFE?
======================================================================
Yes for a SHADOW trial; a short ENFORCE trial is reasonable only after that, and only on your explicit say-so.
  Step 1 (zero risk, recommended first): set identitySafeguard.mode to "shadow" in config.json and restart. Nothing changes in the app; it writes every decision to
          logs/identity-safeguard.jsonl (title, matched entry, decision, reason). After a few days I read the log with you: count the vetoes and look at every one.
  Step 2 (if the log shows essentially no wrong vetoes - I would want at least 30 vetoes reviewed and 95% or better judged right): mode "enforce" for a limited time, with
          the review screen open as a check. The worst case is bounded: a wrongly vetoed listing loses the Database value (it is then valued like an unknown product, saved,
          shown, and still priced by the market check); nothing is deleted, merged, renamed, repriced or created; setting the mode back to "off" restores today's behaviour
          immediately (restart).
  Not recommended yet: turning on Approve/Reject (databaseReview.decisions), any automatic correction, or using the safeguard to choose a different entry.
